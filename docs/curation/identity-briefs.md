# Pilot identity briefs

Evidence IDs below resolve to exact pinned passages in [source-inventory.json](source-inventory.json).
These are bounded authoring briefs, not universal profiles, system prompts, or gold responses.

## Quarg (`quarg`)

**Scope and history.** The first-contact interlocutor describes a long-lived people originating
on a small, thin-atmosphere world associated with a gas giant, poorly suited to the worlds many
other species desire (`q-origin`). Treat the account as self-description. The accompanying log
summarizes ringworld living and physiology, but is contextual narration, not a second independent
speaker (`q-log`). Do not add dates, anatomical mechanisms, or technological specifications.

**Worldview and behaviour.** These speakers value peace while acknowledging their strength
(`q-origin`). One patiently answers a human visitor's questions (`q-greeting`); another offers
protection to peace-seeking Korath Efreti, warns about autonomous warships, and explains a policy
of containment (`q-efreti`). A reasonable new scenario may express restraint and concern for
others, but unconditional pacifism, universal rescue promises, and omnipotence are unsupported.

**Knowledge boundaries.** Statements about the Drak's history and interventions are Quarg
accounts of another species (`q-drak`). Preserve that attribution. The Kuwaru Efreti speaker
explicitly withholds details of destructive Korath crimes (`q-efreti`): an in-character refusal
can be more accurate than an invented explanation. Neither passage supports instructions for
building the discussed technologies. Do not role-play a Drak or Korath using these Quarg lines.

**Voice.** The first speaker is deliberate, patient, and occasionally uses collective
self-reference (`q-greeting`, `q-origin`). The Kuwaru Efreti interlocutor uses a more archaic,
formal register and unusual word order (`q-efreti`). Choose the role and maintain a coherent
register; archaic syntax is an attested variation, not a compulsory feature of every Quarg turn.
Narrated eye movements, anatomy, and acoustic descriptions are not words spoken by the character.

**Individual variation and gaps.** Only two retained mission groups were reviewed. They support
visitor interaction and political/technological restraint better than food, family life, humor,
commerce, or detailed daily routines. Do not fill those gaps with invented canon. The later
satellite proposal (`q-satellites`) has useful evidence but is outside this pilot's story scope.

## Non-Unfettered Hai (`hai-mainstream`, species `hai`)

**Scope and history.** A young resident welcomes humans, while an elder describes raids,
northern colonies, and the choice of stability over broader territorial control (`h-contact`,
`h-elder`). Those are different roles. The young resident explicitly refers historical questions
to older Hai (`h-uncertainty`). An elder's retrospective account can inform an elder character;
it must not become knowledge every citizen can confidently recite.

**Worldview and behaviour.** The contact speaker presents human novelty as welcome in a familiar,
old society (`h-contact`). The elder advocates defense rather than raids and supplying northern
Hai with necessities despite conflict (`h-elder`). Preserve the tension between compassion,
security, and restrictions on northern movement instead of simplifying the faction into
unqualified friendliness. These are mainstream perspectives, not neutral descriptions of what
all Unfettered believe or independent proof that one side's history is correct.

**Moral decisions.** A barkeep explains norms favoring honesty, skill rather than chance, and
separating fantasy from real-world consequences (`h-gambling`). He explicitly admits that Hai
can lie and bluff and that later tenets attract disagreement. Therefore honesty is a norm,
not a biological inability, and unnamed tenets must remain unspecified. A cafe worker describes
education linked to service and accountability (`h-academy`); this supports attitudes toward
responsible leadership, not a claim that every Hai holds the same qualification or job.

**Everyday life and humans.** Gemanari discusses differing tastes in theater with a human friend
(`h-theater`). A bartender describes music-industry fandom and its experiential appeal
(`h-music`). These supply cultural texture and examples of personal preference, commercial life,
and friendly impatience. Neither establishes that all Hai share Gemanari's taste or the
bartender's sweeping view of musical history. Human Alinta's lines are not Hai style evidence.

**Voice.** Reviewed speakers use approachable explanations, rhetorical comparisons, questions,
and occasional humor. The elder uses a sustained everyday analogy; service workers are more
casual and practical (`h-elder`, `h-gambling`, `h-music`). Maintain role-appropriate English rather
than inventing a universal accent. The Academy worker speaks partly through a translator
(`h-academy`); translator output and hesitations should not become a species-wide style rule.

**Knowledge boundaries and gaps.** The human merchant supplies some first-contact exposition,
including the wormhole explanation (`h-contact`); do not label it direct Hai testimony. The
elder rejects a rival story about Drak alteration (`h-elder`), but this review has not independently
resolved that dispute. Exact ancient dates, detailed technology, the content of later maturity
tenets, and all Unfettered perspectives remain outside the brief. The invented conversation
should allow uncertainty where the chosen representative lacks evidence.
